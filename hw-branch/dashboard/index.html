Which harvest day earns the most

Expected net profit per m² per day of growing, the challenge's own score, for each possible harvest day as forecast on the chosen day. A pot sells only when at least a third of its fruit is red, red fruit above 150 g per pot earns nothing extra, and each extra day adds lamp, heating, CO₂ and greenhouse costs. Plants ripen unevenly, so the forecast spreads each room's plants as much as the 5 Nov weighings did. Sale price assumed €2.15 per pot, backed out from the published results; the best day stays the same anywhere between €1.75 and €2.50.

How these numbers were made, and their limits
  • % red by weight: red fruit weight ÷ all fruit weight per plant, averaged per room. Each check cut different plants, so no plant is followed through time.
  • At harvest, plants 31–50 were weighed for red fruit only, so % red at harvest uses plants 1–30 (1–21 for Reference).
  • Photo index: share of pixels that are strongly saturated red after white-balancing each photo. It sees only the top of the canopy and only part of one table, so it shows when ripening starts and how fast it moves within a room, not a room's exact % red.
  • Photos taken on or after harvest day show an empty table and are left out.
  • Degree-days use a 10 °C base, a common textbook value for tomato.
  • Profit: the challenge scored growers on net profit per m² per day. Pots under one-third red are unsellable; red fruit above 150 g per pot adds nothing; each plant costs €0.75 and each re-spacing €1.50/m². Costs to date come from the greenhouse log, future days at the last 7 days' cost rate. The €2.15 price per pot is backed out from the paper's published profits (implied €1.97–2.31 across rooms). Fruit dry matter, which also set the price, is not in the data. Profits computed at each actual harvest rank the rooms almost the same as the published results (rank correlation 0.93).
  • Forecast: % red follows an S-curve in degree-days. "Warmth only" uses one curve for all rooms; "warmth + photos" starts each room's curve from the day red first showed in its photos. Each room is forecast with curves fitted on the other five rooms only. Future warmth is assumed to continue at the last 7 days' rate. The range covers how much rooms differed from each other (95%).
  • Only 6 rooms and 4 measurement days each: patterns here are associations, not proof that one setting caused a result.
