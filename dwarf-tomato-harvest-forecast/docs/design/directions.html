Five directions from the grower's own world

Each direction starts from something growers, packers or buyers already read every day, then applies it to the same fragment of our app: the best harvest day for one room, with the profit window and its gain and loss sides. Pick one to lock, or name the parts to combine. Only then do we write DESIGN.md.

Direction 1 · Source: tomato colour grading

Ripeness scale

The USDA grade standard sorts tomatoes into six colour stages: green, breakers, turning, pink, light red, red. Graders and packers already think in this scale. We make it the backbone: ripeness is always shown as a six-step bar, never a vague gradient, and large plain type sits on a warm, matte card-stock ground.

  • Signature: the six-segment ripeness bar with the current stage outlined.
  • Type: Atkinson Hyperlegible, designed for low-vision legibility.
FitsSpeaks the industry's language; ripeness reads at a glance.WatchRed means "ripe" here, so gain and loss need a second cue (+/− and position), not only colour.

Sources: USDA AMS tomato grade standard · 7 CFR §51 colour classification

Direction 2 · Source: the flower auction clock

Harvest clock

At the Dutch flower auction a single dial counts the price down and every buyer watches one moment. Our decision is the same shape: value rises, peaks, then drains away. The hero is a dial: a green arc from "sellable" to "best day", a red arc after "losses start", and a hand at today. Tall condensed numerals on a deep-night ground.

  • Signature: the harvest dial, one per room, readable from across a room.
  • Type: Barlow Condensed for numbers, Plex Sans for words.
FitsTurns the profit curve into one glanceable object; strong in a video.WatchA dial hides the size of the gain; keep the profit chart one tap away.

Sources: How the auction clocks work (Thursd) · Royal FloraHolland on the clock

Direction 3 · Source: greenhouse climate computers

Grower's console

Growers already spend their day in climate-computer screens: grids of readings against setpoints, trend lines with target bands, deviations flagged, a "percentage of the day in range" score. We borrow that discipline: a calm ruled grid, monospaced readings with aligned digits, and colour only where something drifts from target.

  • Signature: reading vs target in every cell; deviations in amber.
  • Type: IBM Plex Sans and Plex Mono.
FitsFeels like a professional tool growers trust; dense but quiet.WatchCan look technical to an executive; needs a plain-English headline above the grid.

Sources: Priva Operator leaflet · Ridder Synopta · LetsGrow climate monitor (inspiration only; we copy no vendor's look)

Direction 4 · Source: plant tags and crate labels

Crate tag

Plant tags and pallet labels are built to be read in a hurry, in the wet, at arm's length. GS1 logistic labels stack three blocks: who and what at the top, the key data in large human-readable type in the middle, the machine code at the bottom. Each room becomes a tag: heavy black rules, a punched hole, one huge date, a stamp for the decision.

  • Signature: the room tag, stacking who, then when, then the evidence.
  • Type: Barlow Condensed, heavy, upper-case labels.
FitsPhysical, rugged and memorable; connects naturally to the delivery check (crates, dispatch).WatchHeavy black rules need care in dark mode; keep charts light inside it.

Sources: GS1 Logistic Label Guideline · GS1 UK label how-to · UF/IFAS plant label formats · Thrive on clear plant labels

Direction 5 · Source: all-weather field notebooks

Field log

Agronomists and scouts write in all-weather notebooks: a printed grid, tinted grey pages to cut glare, bright orange covers so they can be found in the field. The evidence trail becomes a dated log: each day an entry with the photo taped in, the sample, a tick or a flag. Calm, human, and honest about what was observed versus estimated.

  • Signature: the dated log entry with photo, sample and checkmarks.
  • Type: Plex Mono for entries, Plex Sans for headlines; orange index tabs.
FitsMakes the audit trail feel natural; strong for "why should I trust this".WatchCan feel informal for executives; best for the evidence screen, not the whole app.

Sources: Universal grid page layout · All-weather notebooks overview · Blaze-orange field cover

Crate tags (home): one tag per room, the date is the biggest thing on screen, the stamp says when. Ripeness appears only as the six-step bar.
Harvest day strip (room hero): one tile per day from today: plain before pots sell, green while waiting pays, light blue for the optimal zone (within 10% of best profit), gold on the most-profit day, red once losses mount. Pins mark the shipping, profit and look days.
Big KPI tiles: three numbers, each with a "how is this worked out?" link to the logic, inputs and formula.
Profit chart: green where waiting pays, light-blue optimal zone around the peak, red where each day costs; three harvest points (ship, profit, look); one-line tooltip with a "details" link.
Field log (evidence): the day's photo taped in, with ticks for what was checked.
Console grid: the faint ruled background and hairline cells hold it all together.

How these could combine

The directions aren't exclusive. One workable mix: the crate tag for room cards and the delivery check, a harvest day strip as the hero on each room page (replacing the clock), the ripeness scale wherever ripeness appears, and the field log for the evidence trail, all on the quiet ruled grid of the grower's console. Whatever is chosen, green and red stay reserved for gain and loss, and every number keeps its source.

Research gaps: the USDA stage colours above are approximations of the grading photos, not official values; no vendor interface was copied, and vendor pages are used only as evidence of what growers look at.
